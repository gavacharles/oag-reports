# The Geography of Infrastructure Disputes

**Working title:** The Geography of Infrastructure Disputes: A Spatio-Temporal Analysis of Delay, Cost Overrun and Claims Drivers in Uganda's Public Projects, 2017–2025

**Target journals (Q1 confirmed, September 2026):** International Journal of Project Management; Journal of Management in Engineering (ASCE); Engineering, Construction and Architectural Management

## Research questions
1. Where do dispute drivers concentrate?
2. How has their pattern shifted over 2017–2025, including across the UNRA-to-MoWT transition?
3. Which place-based factors explain the clustering?

## Data lineage (this folder)

Each numbered folder is a pipeline stage, in order. Everything traces back to the raw OAG audit-report PDFs in `../government_Auditor_General/cache/` (shared source material, not duplicated here since it also feeds the original text-mining/ML paper).

- **`01_corpus_audit/`** — Found and quantified document front-matter contamination (table-of-contents dot-leaders, glossary/abbreviations pages, table captions) in the original 1,233-sentence corpus: 12.8% of rows were noise, not audit findings. Verified by spot-check. This stage's cleaned output was superseded by `02_full_reextraction/` once a second, bigger problem was found (see below) — kept for the audit trail.
- **`02_full_reextraction/`** — Found that the original extraction pipeline capped PDF text extraction at 120 pages per report, while actual reports run 164–784 pages (on average 70–85% of each report's content was never read). Re-extracted full text from all 8 cached PDFs (no network calls), applied front-matter stripping up front, and redesigned the driver taxonomy: added `delay_time_overrun` as its own category (didn't exist before — `delayed_payments` is financial, not construction schedule delay), broadened `cost_overrun` and `claims_and_disputes` patterns, made classification multi-label. Result: 1,762 driver-labeled sentences (up from 1,233); `cost_overrun` went from 0 sentences to 34.
- **`03_relevance_filter/`** — Found the infra-relevance filter accepted generic words (`delayed`, `project`, `contract`, `power`) as sufficient alone, letting non-construction content through (academic staffing reports, drug procurement, research-project delays). Added a `construction_relevant` flag requiring a specific infrastructure noun, a named infrastructure agency, or construction-contract-specific terminology. 792/1,762 (44.9%) of driver-labeled sentences pass — this is the corpus the paper's findings should be built on.
- **`04_project_extraction_geocoding/`** — A first regex-based attempt to extract named projects failed in instructive ways (e.g. extracted "Uganda" as a road project because "Uganda Road Fund" matched the same surface pattern as a real project name) — kept in `superseded_regex_attempt/` as a methods-section example of why a reading-based approach was used instead. The reading-based extraction found that only 217/792 sentences (27.4%) name a specific, identifiable project; these canonicalize to **283 distinct projects**, of which **238 were successfully geocoded** via OpenStreetMap Nominatim (212 points, 46 road/transmission-line segments as straight-line proxies between named endpoints). Full methodology, limitations, and reproducibility caveats in `project_extraction_and_geocoding_writeup.md`.
- **`05_covariates/`** — District assignment via spatial join against current OCHA COD-AB boundaries (135 districts), cross-checked against the reading-extracted district (68% agreement; disagreements traced to Nominatim matching same-named villages in the wrong district, e.g. "Luwero Fruit Factory" geocoded to Bugiri — the reading-extracted value is preferred as `district_final`). Distance to Kampala, election-year flags (only Jan 2021 falls in-window; 2025 flagged as the pre-Jan-2026-election run-up). District population/rurality/healthcare-access/Rural Access Index (OCHA COD-PS + HeiGIT/HOT risk-assessment layers, real downloaded data). Point elevation + road-endpoint elevation difference (SRTM via Open-Elevation). CHIRPS mean annual rainfall (2018–2022 climatological baseline, 60 monthly rasters, real point extraction). Land tenure: no GIS layer exists publicly for Uganda — encoded a documented mailo/freehold/customary sub-region proxy instead, cited to the Constitution/Land Act and flagged plainly as a first-pass proxy. Contractor origin / funding source: attempted with the same reading-based approach, but coverage is very low (contractor_origin identified for 6/283 projects, funding_category for 8/283) — OAG narrative rarely names contractors/funders explicitly; this is reported as a data-limitations finding, not used as a regression covariate given the sample size (`05_contractor_funding_writeup.md`).
- **`06_esda/`** — Global Moran's I: significant spatial clustering (p<0.01) for 5 of 8 driver categories, strongest for delayed_payments (I=0.336) and land_and_right_of_way (I=0.333) — **dispute drivers are not spatially random**. Local Getis-Ord Gi* hot spots mapped per category (`hotspot_maps.png`): land/delay cluster in the Kampala-Wakiso-Mukono-Luwero corridor; cost overrun clusters separately in the Hoima/Buliisa oil region and the Mbale/Manafwa/Kween Elgon highlands. District-level spatial regression (OLS → spatial lag/error, 135 districts): covariates fully absorb residual spatial autocorrelation for 2 of 3 tested outcomes (plain OLS adequate); delay_time_overrun rises with distance-to-Kampala and the mailo-tenure proxy, falls with dependency ratio. RF+SHAP robustness check mostly confirms this but flags one real disagreement (direction of distance-to-Kampala's effect on delay) given that model's near-zero out-of-sample R² — reported honestly, not resolved by picking a side. `08_robustness_checks.py` adds alternative spatial weights (Queen/KNN-5/KNN-8) and a negative-binomial GLM per outcome: delay and cost-overrun clustering is robust to weights choice, but land/right-of-way's Moran's I is not robust to KNN-5, and land's OLS-significant population effect vanishes under the count specification — both reported as genuine, unresolved disagreements. Full write-up: `RESULTS_SUMMARY.md`.
- **`07_visualization/`** — National distribution map (all 238 geocoded projects, colored by primary driver) and the Getis-Ord Gi* hot-spot maps, built with a colorblind-safe (CVD-validated) categorical/sequential palette.
- **`08_gwr/`** — Geographically weighted regression (adaptive bisquare kernel, golden-section AICc bandwidth selection). For overall dispute intensity the selected bandwidth uses 133 of 135 districts — statistically indistinguishable from a global model. For land/right-of-way specifically, GWR does improve on the global fit (ΔAICc = 4.25) and reveals an east–west gradient in how distance-to-Kampala and mailo tenure relate to dispute intensity — mapped in `03_gwr_maps.py`'s output.
- **`09_space_time/`** — Annual district-level panel (2017–2025) and an animated Getis-Ord Gi* hot-spot GIF showing the corridor's persistence and the emergence/fade of secondary clusters year by year.
- **`10_manuscript/`** — The full manuscript (`Geography_of_Infrastructure_Disputes_DRAFT.docx`, built programmatically by `build_manuscript.py`), written to stand alone (no dependency on the companion Observer-corpus paper), covering Introduction, Literature/Theory, Data, Methods, Results (national distribution, ESDA, space-time incl. the UNRA-to-MoWT transition, triangulation, spatial regression, GWR, RF/SHAP, robustness checks), Discussion, Implications/Conclusion, Supplementary Material, References (11 verified citations), and an Appendix with the full classifier term lists.
- **`11_triangulation/`** — Two follow-up analyses answering "what else can this data show?" (1) **Triangulation** (`01_observer_triangulation.py`): compared district-level dispute intensity against the companion Observer-newspaper corpus's independent article-mention counts across the 16 districts both sources cover. Spearman ρ = 0.369 (p = 0.160) overall; both sources agree emphatically that Kampala and Wakiso dominate, but ρ collapses to 0.051 (p = 0.861) once those two districts are excluded — real external validation for the headline concentration, real caution against the finer district ranking. (2) **UNRA-to-MoWT difference-in-differences** (`02_unra_mowt_transition.py`): road-sector disputes rose 5.25→8.00/yr (+52%) while every other sector fell 35.50→22.20/yr (-37%) across the identical 2017–2020 vs. 2021–2025 window bracketing UNRA's 2021 dissolution announcement (χ²=8.42, p=0.0037) — a real, precisely-dated, sector-specific pattern whose causal direction (transition → disruption, vs. disputes → political pressure for dissolution, vs. scrutiny → attention reallocation) this single-source design cannot adjudicate; all three readings are stated explicitly in the write-up and the manuscript rather than the more publication-friendly one being picked.
- **`12_media_vs_audit_framing/`** — Exploratory, not yet integrated into the manuscript: does government audit and independent media agree not just on *where* disputes cluster (11_triangulation already answered that) but on *which kind* matters, and on *timing*? (1) **Category framing** (`01_category_framing_comparison.py`): compared the six dispute categories both classifiers share. Even after correcting each source's raw counts for its own validated classifier precision (OAG macro-F1=0.627; Observer's own gold-standard check gives macro-F1=0.231, with its dominant `land_row_dispute` category at precision=0.08), the two sources' category emphasis is essentially uncorrelated (Spearman ρ=-0.03 to -0.09 across 6 shared categories): Observer's coverage is dominated by land/right-of-way disputes (44% of its corrected shared-category volume) while OAG audits are dominated by procurement and contract-management findings (28% and 19%) — audits and media are not describing the same "average" dispute. The category-scheme mismatch itself is informative too: OAG has categories for cost overruns and formal claims/arbitration that Observer's classifier never develops; Observer has a physical build-quality category (`quality_technical_defect`) that OAG's classification scheme doesn't surface separately. (2) **Volume/lead-lag** (`02_volume_lead_lag.py`): compared annual OAG report-year sentence volume against Observer's real-time annual publication volume (n=6 usable years — 2020/2021 excluded as non-standard OAG document types, 2017 has no corpus content). No lead-lag relationship survives (Observer(Y-1) vs OAG(Y): ρ=0.03, p=0.96 — exactly null), but there is a striking negative *contemporaneous* relationship (ρ=-0.83 to -0.89) that survives normalizing for report page count — years of heavier OAG dispute-reporting tend to be years of lighter Observer dispute-coverage, and vice versa. Reported as a genuinely open, small-sample (n=6) descriptive observation, explicitly not a claim, given the total absence of multiple-comparison correction across this folder's several tests.

## Classifier validation (gold-standard check)
`03_relevance_filter/gold_standard/` holds a disclosed, single-AI-rater validation of the driver classifier against 362 independently coded sentences (stratified sample, seed 42; codebook and per-sentence gold labels included). **Macro-F1 = 0.627** (macro-precision 0.711, macro-recall 0.624). Strongest: `land_and_right_of_way` (F1=0.838) and `procurement_irregularities` (F1=0.780). Weakest, from severe over-triggering on generic boilerplate: `governance_and_controls` (P=0.33) and `delayed_payments` (P=0.39) — roughly a third to a half of what the classifier flags in each is, on independent reading, not actually about that construct. **Most consequential finding: `delay_time_overrun` has perfect precision (1.00) but only 0.40 recall** — every flagged delay sentence is genuinely about delay, but the narrow regex misses most delay content because it's usually narrated through procurement/land/payment/contract language instead of its own literal terms. This means the paper's delay-driver counts are very likely undercounted by more than half — reported transparently in the manuscript (Section 3.4, Table 3; also flagged in Limitations and Future Work) rather than left implicit. This is a single-rater AI-assisted validation, not human inter-coder reliability — no kappa is claimed.

## Known limitations to carry into the paper
- Audit coverage is not a census of disputes — OAG doesn't audit every project every year.
- The project-extraction step (04) is reading-based, not a deterministic script or trained NER model: auditable (every row traces to a source sentence with a confidence rating) but not mechanically reproducible.
- The driver classifier itself has an uneven, now-quantified error profile (see "Classifier validation" above) — delay counts are a conservative undercount; governance/oversight and delayed-payments counts are inflated by generic-keyword over-triggering.
- Road/transmission-line geometries are straight-line proxies between named endpoints, not real alignments (would need OSM way-matching or MoWT GIS data).
- Geocoding precision varies by row (`point-exact` / `place-centroid` / `district-centroid` / `failed`) — see `project_level_geocoded.csv`'s `geocode_precision` column before using a given project's coordinates at face value.
- Land tenure is a documented regional proxy, not a parcel- or district-level GIS layer — no such dataset exists publicly.
- Contractor origin and funding source have very low coverage (2–4%) and should be discussed qualitatively/as case examples, not treated as a quantitative covariate.
- The n=135 district regression is small; results are exploratory associations, not causal claims. Two disagreements are flagged rather than papered over: linear-vs-RF on distance-to-Kampala's effect on delay, and OLS-vs-negative-binomial on land/right-of-way's population effect.
- The UNRA-to-MoWT DiD result (Section 11) is a real, significant, precisely-dated pattern but the single-source observational design cannot distinguish which of three plausible causal directions produced it.
